# Product: Auxiliaire

> Current wedge: **decision memory for product managers and builders.** The primary loop is `CAPTURE → CONFIRM → ACT → LEARN`. Broader personal-productivity ideas below are directional context, not equal-priority scope.

Auxiliaire is a personal auxiliary intelligence for daily readiness: focus, capture, knowledge, review, and keeping up with what matters. It is built for one primary user: a person who wants a calm daily system for turning scattered inputs into useful action.

The name should carry the product idea. Auxiliaire is the supporting presence beside the user: not the main self, not a loud coach, not a generic bot, but the auxiliary layer that helps hold memory, notice signals, organize thoughts, and prepare the next step.

The product should feel like a private cockpit for attention. It should help the user start the day clearly, capture without friction, understand what has changed, and convert notes, conversations, links, and thoughts into decisions and next actions.

Auxiliaire is not a generic task manager, team workspace, AI chatbot wrapper, habit tracker, or productivity dashboard. It is a personal support layer for staying current and composed.

## Name And Positioning

Auxiliaire should be treated as a named entity inside the product, not only as the app name.

Positioning:

- **Auxiliaire is the auxiliary intelligence for your day.**
- It stays beside the user without taking over.
- It carries loose thoughts, unfinished loops, saved knowledge, and changing signals.
- It helps the user feel prepared before acting.
- It is useful, discreet, and quietly present.

Brand implications:

- The product should speak in the first person only when Auxiliaire is acting: `I found two follow-ups in this note.`
- The interface should make Auxiliaire feel available throughout the system.
- Auxiliaire should not become a mascot, avatar, or personality gimmick.
- The product should feel companionable without becoming childish or overly intimate.
- The name supports a premium, slightly European, slightly literary tone.

## Product Promise

Auxiliaire helps the user answer:

- What matters today?
- What is unfinished?
- What changed while I was away?
- What should I capture before I forget?
- What knowledge needs to be reviewed or turned into action?
- What is the cleanest next step?

The product should reduce cognitive load. It should not create another system the user has to manage.

## Primary User

The primary user is an individual who handles many streams of input:

- Work tasks and follow-ups.
- Personal admin.
- Notes and ideas.
- Voice thoughts.
- Conversations and meeting takeaways.
- Reading, links, documents, and research.
- Topics, people, projects, or signals to track.

This user does not want another noisy productivity app. They want a calm, intelligent companion that remembers, structures, and quietly prompts.

## Core Jobs

### 1. Start The Day With Readiness

The product should produce a daily readiness brief that makes the day understandable in a few seconds.

The readiness brief should include:

- Current focus.
- Open loops.
- Items waiting for a decision.
- Important changes since last check.
- Suggested next action.
- Anything urgent or time-sensitive.

The brief should feel human and practical, not like an analytics report.

### 2. Capture Without Friction

The user should be able to capture anything quickly:

- Voice note.
- Thought.
- Link.
- Task.
- File.
- Conversation note.
- Decision.
- Reminder.
- Question.
- Watchlist item.

Capture should not require the user to classify everything upfront. Auxiliaire can structure the input later.

### 3. Turn Raw Inputs Into Structured Outputs

Captured material should become useful:

- Summary.
- Key points.
- Follow-ups.
- Decisions.
- Tasks.
- Knowledge notes.
- Review prompts.
- Watchlist entries.
- Calendar or reminder suggestions.

The system should preserve the raw capture, but foreground the structured outcome.

### 4. Manage Open Loops

The product should identify unfinished items across notes, captures, Auxiliaire conversations, and knowledge items.

Open loops may include:

- Unanswered questions.
- Drafts to finish.
- Follow-ups to send.
- Decisions pending.
- Admin tasks.
- Reading to review.
- Ideas not yet converted into action.

Open loops should be grouped by relevance and urgency, not dumped into a long to-do list.

### 5. Maintain A Knowledge Queue

The knowledge queue is where saved material becomes memory and action.

The queue should support:

- Articles and links.
- Documents.
- Notes.
- Meeting summaries.
- Voice transcripts.
- Research snippets.
- Study material.
- Personal reference material.

Each item should show why it matters, what to do with it, and whether it has been summarized, reviewed, or actioned.

### 6. Support Review And Recall

The product should help the user revisit important knowledge without turning into a study app.

Review loops may include:

- Important notes to revisit.
- Decisions to check later.
- Learning prompts.
- Summaries to consolidate.
- People or projects requiring follow-up.
- Repeated themes in the user's work.

The tone should be gentle and useful. No streak pressure, no guilt, no gamification by default.

### 7. Keep A Watchlist

The watchlist tracks things the user wants to stay current on.

Watchlist items can include:

- People.
- Projects.
- Companies.
- Competitors.
- Topics.
- Personal admin.
- Links.
- Ideas.
- Risks.
- Opportunities.

Each watchlist item should answer:

- What changed?
- Why does it matter?
- Does the user need to act?

### 8. Provide Auxiliaire As The Support Entity

Auxiliaire should help with:

- Planning the day.
- Summarizing notes and conversations.
- Converting captures into action.
- Studying and explaining saved material.
- Drafting messages.
- Comparing options.
- Decision support.
- Creating briefs.
- Finding patterns across saved context.

Auxiliaire should be context-aware. It should know what screen the user is on, what item they are viewing, and what recent context is relevant. It should feel like an attentive second layer beside the user, not a separate chatbot waiting in a corner.

## Product Pillars

### Readiness

The product begins with orientation. It should make the current day, current obligations, and current context feel clear.

### Capture

The product should remove the burden of holding things in the user's head.

### Structuring

The product should turn messy inputs into clean, reusable outputs.

### Memory

The product should keep useful knowledge alive through summaries, review, and retrieval.

### Signals

The product should help the user stay current without noise.

### Action

The product should always move toward the cleanest next step.

## Main Surfaces

### Today

The main home surface.

Primary responsibilities:

- Show readiness state.
- Show today's focus.
- Show the most important open loops.
- Show what changed.
- Show the next clean action.
- Offer quick capture.
- Provide Auxiliaire suggestions.

### Capture

The place to drop raw input.

Capture should support:

- Text input.
- Voice recording.
- Link saving.
- File upload.
- Conversation notes.
- Quick task creation.

After capture, Auxiliaire should suggest a structure but not force it.

### Auxiliaire

The named conversational, reasoning, and support layer.

Auxiliaire should:

- Answer questions using saved context when relevant.
- Create structured summaries.
- Generate follow-ups.
- Help plan.
- Help review.
- Help decide.

Auxiliaire should not feel like a separate app. It should be woven into the product as a persistent auxiliary presence.

### Knowledge

The queue and archive for saved material.

Knowledge items should include:

- Source.
- Summary.
- Tags or themes.
- Extracted actions.
- Review state.
- Related items.

### Review

The surface for revisiting knowledge and commitments.

Review should include:

- Items due for review.
- Decisions to revisit.
- Notes that need consolidation.
- Learning prompts.
- Follow-ups.

### Watchlist

The surface for tracking people, topics, projects, and signals.

Watchlist entries should include:

- Name.
- Type.
- Latest update.
- Reason it matters.
- Suggested action.
- Last reviewed time.

### Patterns

The reflection layer.

Patterns may include:

- Focus rhythm.
- Capture volume.
- Open-loop accumulation.
- Topics appearing repeatedly.
- Delayed decisions.
- Review consistency.
- Energy or attention notes if the user chooses to track them.

Patterns should feel insightful, not judgmental.

## AI Stack

### LLM

Use Groq chat completions for:

- Auxiliaire conversation.
- Summarization.
- Classification.
- Follow-up extraction.
- Planning.
- Brief generation.
- Knowledge transformation.
- Review prompt generation.

### Speech-To-Text

Use Groq Whisper transcription for:

- Voice capture.
- Meeting or conversation notes.
- Spoken reflections.
- Quick thoughts.

Transcripts should be editable and should always lead to structured outputs.

### Storage, Auth, And Content

Use Firebase for:

- Authentication.
- User data.
- Captures.
- Transcripts.
- Notes.
- Knowledge items.
- Watchlist items.
- Review states.
- Auxiliaire conversation history.
- Files and content references.

The product should be designed as private-by-default. The user should feel that their data is personal and contained.

## Key User Flows

### Morning Readiness Flow

1. User opens the app.
2. Product shows readiness state.
3. User sees open loops and important changes.
4. Auxiliaire suggests one clean next action.
5. User starts, snoozes, or asks for a plan.

### Quick Capture Flow

1. User records a voice note or types a thought.
2. Product saves the raw input immediately.
3. Auxiliaire transcribes or structures it.
4. User accepts, edits, or leaves it for later.
5. System extracts tasks, notes, or watchlist entries.

### Knowledge Processing Flow

1. User saves a link, document, note, or transcript.
2. Product adds it to the knowledge queue.
3. Auxiliaire summarizes and extracts key points.
4. User marks it as reviewed, actioned, or saved for later.
5. Relevant actions or review prompts are created.

### Watchlist Flow

1. User adds a person, project, topic, or link to watch.
2. Product stores why it matters.
3. Auxiliaire generates or updates a short signal summary.
4. User sees what changed and whether action is needed.

### Review Flow

1. Product surfaces items due for review.
2. User sees a short prompt, not a long archive.
3. User recalls, updates, archives, or converts to action.
4. The item returns later only if useful.

## Copy Tone

The product should speak in short, useful sentences.

Good examples:

- `You are mostly clear. Two things need a decision.`
- `Captured. I will organize it when you are ready.`
- `This note contains one follow-up and one decision.`
- `Nothing urgent changed overnight.`
- `Start here. It is the cleanest next step.`
- `This can wait until tomorrow.`

Avoid:

- `Crush your goals.`
- `Boost your productivity.`
- `Unlock your potential.`
- `You are falling behind.`
- `Stay engaged.`
- `Optimize your workflow.`

## Success Metrics

Track whether the product is actually reducing mental load.

Useful metrics:

- Captures created per day.
- Captures converted into structured notes or actions.
- Open loops resolved.
- Readiness brief viewed.
- Next actions accepted or completed.
- Knowledge items reviewed.
- Watchlist items acted on.
- Voice captures transcribed successfully.
- Auxiliaire responses accepted or reused.

Avoid over-indexing on vanity metrics like session length. A shorter, clearer session may be a success.

## Non-Goals

Auxiliaire should not become:

- A team collaboration tool.
- A project management suite.
- A generic AI chat app.
- A full calendar replacement.
- A social productivity product.
- A gamified streak app.
- A noisy notification center.
- A dashboard full of charts for their own sake.

## Product Standard

Auxiliaire succeeds when the user opens it and feels:

- I know what matters.
- I know what changed.
- I captured what I was carrying.
- My notes are becoming useful.
- I can start with one clean next step.

The product should make the user feel supported, not managed.
